County Board Approves $24 Million Jasper Infrastructure Overhaul

By Sarah Mitchell April 8, 2026 5 min read
The Cottonwood County Courthouse in Windom. (File photo)

The Cottonwood County Board of Commissioners voted unanimously Tuesday to approve a sweeping $24 million infrastructure package that will reshape the city of Jasper's water and sewer systems over the next three years.

The project, which has been in planning for more than two years, addresses aging underground infrastructure that county engineers have flagged as a growing public health concern. Several water mains dating to the 1960s have experienced repeated breaks.

"This is the single largest infrastructure investment in Jasper's history," said Board Chair Tom Anderson. "We owe it to the next generation to get this right."

The funding package combines state bonding dollars, federal infrastructure grants, and a modest local levy increase. County Administrator Lisa Chen noted that roughly 60% of the funding comes from outside sources.

Construction is expected to begin in spring 2027, with the first phase focusing on the downtown water main corridor along Main Street. Residents can expect periodic lane closures but no extended road shutdowns.

The board also approved a community outreach plan that includes quarterly town halls and a dedicated project website where residents can track progress and submit concerns.

City Council Approves Downtown Streetscape Plan After Public Input

By Mark Johnson April 7, 2026 4 min read
Winnewissa Falls at Pipestone National Monument. (File photo / Pipestone County Star)

The Pipestone City Council voted 5-1 Monday evening to approve a comprehensive downtown streetscape improvement plan that will bring new sidewalks, decorative lighting, and expanded green space to the historic Main Street corridor.

The $3.2 million project, funded primarily through a combination of state bonding money and local option sales tax revenue, has been the subject of extensive public discussion over the past six months.

Mayor Jennifer Larson called the vote "a turning point for downtown Pipestone" and praised the community engagement process that shaped the final design.

Construction will begin in late summer 2026, starting with the two-block stretch between Hiawatha Avenue and Olive Street. The project is expected to be complete by fall 2027.

The lone dissenting vote came from Councilmember Robert Gustafson, who expressed concerns about the impact on downtown parking during construction. City staff responded that a temporary parking plan would be in place.

Business owners along Main Street have largely expressed support, with several noting that the improvements could help attract new retail tenants to vacant storefronts.
