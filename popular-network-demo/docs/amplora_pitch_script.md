# Amplora — Verbal Pitch Script

*Talking points for a ~5-minute walkthrough with a partner who is seeing this for the first time. Read as written, or use as a riff. `[Pause for reaction]` markers flag natural breakpoints — let the partner respond, ask, push back. Conversational pace is roughly 150 words per minute.*

*Total length: ~750 words ≈ 5 minutes. Add ~2–3 minutes for pause-and-react time.*

---

## Opening (~30 seconds)

> So, before we dig in — I want to walk you through something I've been building. The shape of this matters, and the partner-fit question is going to come up pretty fast. Five minutes, roughly. Stop me whenever something needs unpacking.

`[Pause for reaction.]`

---

## What Amplora is (~45 seconds)

> The product is called Amplora. It's a commerce platform we license to local newspapers — you run it in your territory, we operate the platform from Amplify Media Group.

> There are three products that run on top of it. The first one — what we're shipping in the next 90 days — is an AI Marketing Agent for small local businesses. Three tiers at $99, $299, and $499 a month. Voice interview, social media, Google Business Profile, reviews, hosted website, the whole marketing department, run by AI with owner approval on everything that goes public.

> Inside of a year, the second product layers on: network-amplified display advertising. Ads you sell can ride into other territories' chatbots for a multiplier on your standard rate.

> Year 2, the third product: Tier 4 inventory verticals. Auto dealers, ag equipment, real estate, RV and boat, lumber yards. $799 to $2,000-plus a month, with a real search-engine experience for shoppers.

> One platform. Three products. All sold through the relationships you already have.

`[Pause for reaction.]`

---

## The selling point (~90 seconds)

> Here's the part I want to land the hardest, because this is what makes the whole thing actually work.

> Every Marketing Agent subscription does two things at the same time.

> One: the small business gets their own marketing system. Voice onboarding, social posting, Google profile, reviews, dashboard. Stuff that costs $500 to $2,000 a month at an agency. Stuff that most small-town SMBs literally cannot access right now at any price they can afford. We hand them a real marketing operation, branded as theirs, working every single day. The reason we can do that and an agency can't is — AI does the work. The cost structure is different.

> Two: at the same time, that same subscription pours that business's information into the Amplora chatbot. Their hours, services, posts, photos, reviews, products — all flowing in real time into the chatbot. And the chatbot becomes the local discovery layer for your territory.

> **One subscription. Two outcomes.** Recurring revenue for you. Real value for the customer. And every customer makes the chatbot smarter, which makes the chatbot more useful for consumers, which makes the chatbot more valuable to advertise on, which makes the next customer easier to sign.

> The same pattern repeats at the other two products. Display ads ride your sales motion into the chatbot. Tier 4 inventory becomes searchable in the chatbot. **Every product feeds the chatbot. The chatbot makes every product more valuable.** That's the flywheel.

`[Pause for reaction. Let it land.]`

> A few other things worth flagging quick: the **trust transfer** — you walk into every conversation with credibility a SaaS company can't buy. The **two-sided moat** — publisher relationships plus a chatbot already operating as the local discovery layer; a Silicon Valley competitor can't replicate either one of those, much less both. **Revenue rescue** — we're clawing back marketing dollars these businesses are sending to Mailchimp, AutoTrader, Zillow right now. And **stickiness** — three or four months in, switching costs are real.

`[Pause.]`

---

## What you make (~75 seconds)

> Let me show you the economics from your side.

> Marketing agent subscriptions: 50% to you in year one. 40% on renewals. There's a 60% performance tier when you hit 20 active customers with 80% retention — so the more you grow, the more you keep.

> Display ad reach upsells: when an advertiser pays for network reach, you keep 65% as the selling publisher. Receiving publishers split 25% based on actual chatbot impressions they delivered. The platform takes 10%.

> Tier 4 inventory: 50% in year one, 40% on renewals. A Tier 4 Base customer at $799 a month is roughly $400 a month to you, $4,800 a year — and those customers stick. 36-month average tenure versus 24 for marketing agent.

> The picture I want you to hold is Year 2 or Year 3 — where you're earning rev share from all three product lines at the same time. Year 1, it's mostly marketing agent. Year 2, ad reach starts layering in with no new sales motion. Year 3, Tier 4 dealers start landing — those are the bigger checks. Year-1 ARR target across the network is $105 to $150 thousand. Year 3, $1.35 to $3 million.

`[Pause for reaction.]`

---

## What we'd need from you (~60 seconds)

> The pilot is five customers — three in Windom, two in Pipestone, in Q3. Easy verticals: retail, restaurants, salons, auto repair, home services. Your sales staff makes the calls. I co-sell the first five with you so the motion is muscle memory before you're flying solo.

> The one parallel piece is consumer-side marketing for the chatbot. Without consumers actually using the chatbot, the value chain collapses for all three products. So we run a chatbot adoption push at the same time as the SMB pilot — in-paper promotion, social, partnerships with schools, libraries, civic groups. The target is 1,000 monthly chatbot users in your territory by month three.

> A handful of things we'd need to settle in the next few weeks: brand application — does it say "Amplora" to the customer, or "Pipestone Marketing, powered by Amplora"; who recruits the pilot 5; the publisher contract template; the budget for the consumer-side push.

`[Pause.]`

---

## Close (~30 seconds)

> That's the platform. The thing I keep coming back to — this isn't really an "AI does your marketing" pitch. It's: **your local newspaper is now a commerce platform.** We market your businesses, sell your ads, put your inventory in front of every shopper in the region. The AI is just the cost structure that makes it work.

> What's your gut reaction? Where do you have questions?

---

## If they push back — quick objection map

- *"This sounds like a lot of new tech for our staff to learn."* → The customer-facing experience is a 30-minute voice interview and a thumbs-up/thumbs-down dashboard. Your sales staff sells. Amplify Media Group runs the platform. You don't operate the AI.
- *"What if the AI screws something up?"* → Owner-approval gate on every public piece of content in V1. Plus a quality-monitoring layer that flags weak engagement and a human concierge who reviews flagged work. We do not let AI publish without owner approval in year one.
- *"What if our consumers don't use the chatbot?"* → Real risk. That's why the consumer-launch push runs in parallel with the SMB pilot, not after. We're committing to 1,000+ monthly chatbot users in your territory by month 3 — that's the threshold.
- *"What's the catch on the rev share dropping at renewal?"* → Less work to keep a customer than to win one. And the 60% performance tier is the upside — once you have 20 customers retaining, you're earning more on renewals than the headline 40% suggests.
- *"What happens if a Silicon Valley AI company shows up in our market?"* → They'd have to replicate the publisher-trust layer AND the local-discovery chatbot. Either one alone is a multi-year build. Both together is the moat.
