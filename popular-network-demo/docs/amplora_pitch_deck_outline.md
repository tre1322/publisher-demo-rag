# Amplora — Partner Pitch Deck Outline

*A 13-slide outline for an in-person walkthrough with a publisher partner. Headlines are written declaratively (the slide says the takeaway, not just the topic). Body bullets are presenter-facing; cut/condense for the actual slide. Each slide includes a speaker note for the angle.*

*Estimated talk time: ~15–20 minutes including reaction time.*

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**Amplora**
*A commerce platform for local newspapers.*

— Trevor Slette, Amplify Media Group
— Strategic Partner Conversation, May 2026

> **Speaker note:** Keep this slide up while you and the partner sit down. Let the brand and tagline land before you start talking. Open with: *"I want to walk you through something I've been building. Five minutes of presentation, then I want your gut reaction."*

## Slide 2 — Newspapers are losing revenue to platforms that don't have local trust

- Digital agencies: $500–2,000/month per SMB → away from publishers
- National listing services: AutoTrader, Zillow, Indeed, Realtor.com → away from classifieds
- Big-tech ad platforms: Meta, Google → away from display
- Local SMBs can't afford an agency or a $30–50k/year hire — they limp on stale Facebook pages
- **AI changes the cost structure** — what cost $1,500/month now costs $299

> **Speaker note:** This is the problem framing. Don't dwell — partners feel this every day. The takeaway is the last bullet: AI is the wedge that lets newspapers reclaim this revenue.

---

## Slide 3 — Amplora is the platform that brings that revenue back

> *"Your local newspaper is now a commerce platform. We market your business, sell your ads, and put your inventory in front of every shopper in the region — at a price that actually works for a small-town economy."*

**Three layered products:**
1. AI Marketing Agent (ships first)
2. Network-Amplified Display Advertising (Year 1–2)
3. Tier 4 Inventory Verticals (Year 2–3)

**Operated by Amplify Media Group · Licensed to publisher partners**

> **Speaker note:** This is the one-sentence pitch. Read the quote out loud, slowly. Then frame the three products as layered, not parallel — marketing agent first, others build on it.

## Slide 4 — One subscription does two things at the same time

**This is the selling point.**

| | What the subscriber gets | What the platform gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing Agent subscription** | A real marketing department: voice onboarding, social, GBP, reviews, dashboard | The business's information flowing into the Amplora chatbot |

→ Recurring revenue for the publisher
→ Real value for the small business that otherwise can't access this
→ Moat-building data for the platform

**The same dual-value pattern repeats at all three products:**
*Every product feeds the chatbot. The chatbot makes every product more valuable.*

> **Speaker note:** This is THE slide. Spend the most time here. The two-column table is the visual punch. The "every product feeds the chatbot" line is the flywheel framing.

| **1 — Network Presence** | $99/mo | Full chatbot profile, AI-discoverability files (`/pricing.md` + `/llms.txt`) |

## Slide 6 — Display Ad Reach: incremental revenue with no new sales motion

**Multipliers on your standard display ad rate for chatbot reach into other territories:**

| Reach Tier | Multiplier | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Local | 1.0× (included) | Your publication + your territory's chatbot |
| Regional (50 mi) | 1.5× | + adjacent territories |
| Network (100 mi) | 2.0× | + wider coverage |
| Maximum | 2.5–3.0× | Full network |

**Where it sells:** help-wanted, real estate, auto, ag equipment, professional services with broad geography.

**Where it doesn't:** restaurants, corner stores, hyper-local services — and that's fine.

> **Speaker note:** Stress: same reps, same conversations, just a bigger ticket when the advertiser wants reach. This is layered revenue, not new motion.

**Market context:** AutoTrader Premier ($300–1,000/dealer/mo), Zillow Premier Agent ($300–3,000/agent/mo). Amplora prices competitively while delivering the publisher-relationship moat the national listing services don't have.

**SW Peach territory TAM:** ~80–120 inventory-tier customers.

> **Speaker note:** This is the bigger-check tier. 36-month average tenure. The "search engine" framing matters — it's not a directory, it's a query interface for active shoppers.

## Slide 8 — The Local-First Algorithm protects locals while enabling network reach

**Hard-coded rule in the chatbot:**

1. **Local results always surface first** — any local ad/listing shows above any out-of-territory result
2. Out-of-territory paid-reach results appear only when:
   - (a) The advertiser paid for reach to the user's location, AND
   - (b) Local results don't fully answer the query
3. Distance is always disclosed
4. Users can opt into broader-range results explicitly

**Same rule applies to display ads (Phase 1.5) and inventory (Phase 2).**

> **Speaker note:** This is the structural protection that makes the multi-territory model honest. Partners need to grok this to sell network reach in good conscience. The advertiser-facing version: *"Your ad is seen first by people in your own territory. Network reach is for getting in front of additional eyes after your local audience has been served."*

## Slide 9 — The moat is the publisher + the chatbot, reinforcing each other

- **Trust transfer** — 50+ years of newspaper credibility no SaaS can buy
- **Two-sided moat** — publisher relationships + a chatbot operating as the local discovery layer; a Silicon Valley competitor can't replicate either, much less both
- **Revenue rescue** — clawing back marketing dollars going to Mailchimp, AutoTrader, Zillow
- **AI-discoverability network effect** — every Amplora business gets cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude (`/pricing.md` + `/llms.txt` auto-generated)
- **Stickiness** — 3+ months in, switching costs are real (and higher for Tier 4 dealers)

> **Speaker note:** Don't read all five — pick the two or three that resonate most for this partner. Trust transfer + AI discoverability tend to land hardest with publishers; the two-sided moat is the answer to "what if a competitor shows up?"

## Slide 12 — Top risks and how we're handling each

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Chatbot needs consumer adoption | Aggressive consumer-launch in parallel with SMB pilot; target 1,000+ monthly users by month 3 |
| Publisher has to actually sell | 50% rev share, clear sales kit, performance-tier upside, Trevor co-selling alongside reps in early months |
| AI quality at scale | Quality-monitoring layer + human concierge fallback + owner-approval gate on every public piece in V1 |
| Phase 1.5 cross-territory revenue disputes | Written allocation rules in publisher agreement before launch; transparent attribution dashboard |
| Phase 2 inventory integration cost overruns | Sequence verticals: auto → ag → real estate; no multi-vertical launch in single quarter |

> **Speaker note:** Don't soft-pedal these. Partners trust honesty about risk more than confidence about everything. The mitigations matter more than the risks themselves.
