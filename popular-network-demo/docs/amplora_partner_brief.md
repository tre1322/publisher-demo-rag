## What Amplora is

Amplora is a commerce platform we license to local newspaper publishers in their territories. Three layered products run on top of it:

1. **AI Marketing Agent** for local small businesses (ships first)
2. **Network-amplified display advertising** sold through your existing sales motion (within 12 months)
3. **Tier 4 inventory verticals** for dealers — auto, ag equipment, real estate, RV/boat, lumber (Year 2)

The platform is operated by **Amplify Media Group**. You run it in your territory.

In one sentence: **"Your local newspaper is now a commerce platform. We market your business, sell your ads, and put your inventory in front of every shopper in the region — at a price that actually works for a small-town economy."**

That's the *what*. Here's why it sells.

## The selling point

**Every Marketing Agent subscription does two things at the same time. This is the thing to understand first.**

1. **The small business gets its own marketing system — something most of them otherwise can't access.** A real marketing setup runs $500–2,000/month at an agency or $30–50k/year for a hire. Small-town SMBs can't pay that, so they limp along with a stale Facebook page or a teenager who quit two months ago. We give them what only chains have today: voice-interview onboarding, custom marketing plan, social content drafted/scheduled/posted, full Google Business Profile management, review responses, hosted website, dashboard. AI is what makes the cost structure work.

2. **The same subscription pours that business's information into the Amplora chatbot.** Hours, services, posts, photos, reviews, products, specials — all of it flowing in real time into the chatbot's local-discovery layer. A consumer searching *"best brake shop in Windom"* gets an answer grounded in real, current, local business data — not a Google ad or a dead website.

**One subscription. Two outcomes.** The publisher signs the customer once and earns recurring revenue from a service that simultaneously builds the moat for the next decade.

The same dual-value pattern repeats at the other product lines: display ads ride your sales motion into other territories' chatbots; Tier 4 dealer inventory becomes searchable across the network. **Every product line feeds the chatbot. The chatbot makes every product line more valuable.** That's the flywheel.

### Other things worth flagging

- **Trust transfer.** We arrive in every conversation with credibility a SaaS company can't buy. The local paper has been the trusted small-business advisor in these markets for fifty-plus years.
- **Two-sided moat.** A national AI marketing startup might match the software. They cannot match the publisher relationships AND a chatbot that's already the local discovery layer.
- **Revenue rescue.** The marketing money these businesses spend today — Mailchimp, Constant Contact, freelance social, agencies an hour away, AutoTrader, Zillow, Indeed — used to be print and classified revenue. Amplora claws a chunk back through the channel that lost it.
- **Stickiness.** After three or four months running through the system, switching is painful. Tier 4 dealers have higher switching costs once their inventory feed is integrated and listings are getting traffic.
- **AI-discoverability network effect.** Every Amplora business gets an auto-generated `/pricing.md` and `/llms.txt` — the structured files ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Claude use to cite businesses. Amplora customers get cited across the broader AI assistant ecosystem, not just our chatbot. No competitor in this market is doing this.
- **Forward-looking story for the newspaper.** Something to talk about and grow into for the next decade — not just defense around print.

### Local-First Algorithm — protects locals while enabling network reach

Hard-coded rule: local results always surface first in the chatbot. Out-of-territory paid-reach ads or listings appear only when (a) the advertiser paid for reach to the user's location AND (b) local results don't fully answer the query. Distance is always disclosed; users can opt into broader-range explicitly. Same rule applies to Phase 1.5 ads and Phase 2 inventory. It's how the network grows without cannibalizing local advertisers — and why publishers can sell network reach in good conscience.

## The three product lines

### Marketing Agent (ships first)

Three tiers. Annual contracts with a 30-day opt-out at day 90, $299–499 setup fee credited back at day 90 (so real customers pay no setup; tire-kickers pay it).

- **Tier 1 — Network Presence — $99/month.** Full business profile in the Amplora chatbot. Auto-generated `/pricing.md` + `/llms.txt` for external AI assistants. *(Pilot: $29/month for first 90 days to seed listings.)*
- **Tier 2 — Marketing Agent — $299/month.** Tier 1 + the marketing department: voice interview, custom plan, social posting across Facebook/Instagram, full Google Business Profile management, review monitoring with AI-drafted owner responses, hosted website (or integration), owner approval workflow, monthly performance dashboard.
- **Tier 3 — Marketing Agent + Business Concierge — $499/month.** Tier 2 + dedicated business chatbot (consumers enter deep conversation about that one business), owner SMS/push escalation, monthly competitive intelligence, quarterly business review with publisher rep.

### Display Ad Network Amplification (Year 1–2)

Multipliers on your standard display ad rate for tiered chatbot reach into other territories:

- **Local Reach** — included (1.0×)
- **Regional Reach** (50 mi) — 1.5×
- **Network Reach** (100 mi) — 2.0×
- **Maximum Reach** (full network) — 2.5–3.0×

Where it sells: help-wanted ads, real estate, auto, ag equipment, professional services with broad geography. Simple test: would the advertiser drive customers in from 50 miles? If yes, sell reach. Restaurants and corner stores generally won't — that's fine.

This is **incremental** revenue layered on existing display sales — same reps, same conversations, bigger ticket when the advertiser wants reach.

### Tier 4 Inventory Verticals (Year 2–3)

Auto, ag equipment, real estate, RV/boat/powersports, lumber yards. Dealers whose product is searchable inventory. Listings flow into the chatbot — real photos, prices, availability — and consumers query like a search engine.

- **Tier 4 Base — $799/month** — up to 50 listings, one management-system integration
- **Tier 4 Plus — $1,299/month** — up to 250 listings, two-system integration, multi-location
- **Tier 4 Enterprise — $1,999+/month** — unlimited listings, custom integrations

Annual contracts, $999–2,499 setup fee credited back at day 90. Market context: AutoTrader Premier $300–1,000/dealer/month, Zillow Premier Agent $300–3,000/agent/month. Amplora prices competitively while delivering the publisher-relationship moat the national listing services don't have.

SW Peach territory alone: ~8–15 auto dealers, 3–6 ag dealers, 50+ real estate agents — combined Phase 2 TAM of 80–120 inventory-tier customers in your home territory.

## What you do, and what you make

**You sell, sign, and stay in front of the customer.** Your existing sales staff calls on local businesses with a kit we provide. Marketing agent closes typically take 1–3 conversations because these are warm relationships. Tier 4 inventory is a longer sales cycle — Amplify Media Group brings dedicated solution-sales help when you're ready.

**Revenue share by product line:**

- **Marketing agent subscriptions:** 50% in year one, 40% on renewals, 60% performance tier (20+ active customers, 80%+ retention). 100% of any incremental display advertising you sell on top.
- **Display ad reach upsells:** 65% to selling publisher, 25% split among receiving publishers proportional to chatbot impressions delivered, 10% to platform. Receiving share rewards publishers who actually build chatbot consumer adoption.
- **Tier 4 inventory subscriptions:** 50% in year one, 40% on renewals. A Tier 4 Base customer at $799/month is roughly $400/month to you — and Tier 4 customers stay 36 months on average vs. 24 for marketing agent customers.

Setup fees pass through on the same revenue share. Customer ad spend is pass-through, not revenue. Future media-management fees split 50/50.

## What success looks like

We're not trying to grow fast. We're trying to grow well, in sequence.

- **Year 1:** 5-customer pilot in Windom and Pipestone → open to SW Peach in months 4–8 → 35–50 active marketing agent customers by year-end. **ARR ~$105k–$150k**, plus $5k–$15k incremental from Phase 1.5 launching late.
- **Year 2:** 100–150 marketing agent customers across 2–3 territories. Phase 1.5 fully operational. Phase 2 inventory soft-launches in second half (5–15 Tier 4 customers). **Total ARR ~$380k–$730k.**
- **Year 3:** 300–500 marketing agent customers across 5–7 territories. 30–80 Tier 4 customers. **Total ARR ~$1.35M–$3M.**

The number that matters most early is **retention**. Five pilot customers who renew matter more than fifty who churn.

## Top risks (in human terms)

1. **Chatbot needs consumer adoption.** All three product lines depend on it. Mitigation: aggressive consumer-side launch in parallel with the SMB pilot; target 1,000+ monthly chatbot users in pilot territory by month 3.
2. **Publisher has to actually sell.** Fails if the partnership signs and sales staff doesn't get pushed. Mitigation: 50% rev share, clear sales kit, performance tier upside, Trevor co-selling alongside your reps in the early months.
3. **AI quality at scale.** Content can be off-brand or wrong. Mitigation: quality-monitoring layer, human concierge fallback, owner-approval gate on every public piece in V1.
4. **Phase 1.5 cross-territory revenue disputes.** Mitigation: written allocation rules in publisher agreement before launch, transparent attribution dashboard.
5. **Phase 2 inventory integration cost overruns.** Mitigation: sequence verticals (auto first, then ag, then real estate); no multi-vertical launch in a single quarter.
