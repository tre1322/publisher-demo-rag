# Main Street OS — Pilot Pitch Script

For walking into 5 Windom businesses. Keep it in your pocket, read it twice before the first visit. You don't need to follow it word-for-word — the point is to know which beats to hit so you don't blank.

---

## Core framing (memorize this)

You are **not selling software**. You are **inviting them into a pilot** that helps their customers find them in a world where customers have started asking AI instead of Google. You're the publisher of their local paper; you've already built the AI assistant their community is starting to use; you want them to be one of the first 5 businesses in Windom inside it.

The pitch is about **their customer**, not your tech. Every time you feel yourself explaining features, stop and say what the customer sees instead.

---

## The 30-second version (walking in the door)

> "Hey [Name], got two minutes? I wanted to show you something we're rolling out with the Citizen. You know how more and more people are asking ChatGPT and Google's AI where to go for stuff instead of searching? The problem is those AI tools don't know your business exists yet — only about one percent of local businesses show up in them. We've built our own AI assistant for Windom that knows the whole community, and I'm picking five local businesses to be the first ones featured in it. I thought of you. Can I show you what it looks like?"

**What you just did:**
- Named the change (AI, not Google)
- Framed the threat (customers can't find them)
- Gave specific number (1% — feels real)
- Positioned it as a pilot / scarcity (first 5)
- Flattered them (I thought of you)
- Asked permission to continue

If they say **yes**, pull out your phone/iPad and go to the walkthrough.

If they say **not now**, ask: *"When would be a good time this week? I've only got 5 spots and I'd rather you have one than someone else."* Leave the one-pager.

---

## The 5-minute walkthrough (with your phone/iPad in hand)

### Step 1: Show what their customer sees (60 seconds)

Open the live chatbot. Ask it a real question about their category. For a pizza place:

> *"Watch this. If somebody in Windom pulls up the Citizen's site and asks 'where can I get pizza tonight' — look, here's the answer. Right now it mentions whoever's advertising. With Main Street OS, YOUR answer is the one that shows up, with your hours, your phone, your deal of the week. Every time. For 20 people a month on the Growth plan, 100 on Premium."

**Don't linger on the interface.** They care about being the answer, not the UI.

### Step 2: Show them THEIR dashboard (90 seconds)

Open `/business/` after logging in as a test account (pre-create one before visits so you have real-looking data). Point at the stat cards:

> *"Once you're in, you get this dashboard. It tells you how many times the AI mentioned you this month, how many people clicked through, and — this is the cool part — the actual questions people asked that brought them to you. So if you see 'gluten free pizza near me' came up 14 times, that tells you something nobody else in Windom knows."*

**The insight hook:** They've never seen customer-intent data from AI queries before. Nobody has. This is what makes it feel different from just a directory listing.

### Step 3: Show how easy signup is (30 seconds)

Don't walk them through signup in person. Just describe it:

> *"When you say yes, I'll text you a link. You click it, fill out the basics — your hours, phone, what makes you different — takes about two minutes. Then you write one or two sentences that you want the AI to say when people ask about [their category]. That's it."*

### Step 4: Pull out the one-pager (60 seconds)

Hand them the printed sheet. Don't walk through every line — that's what the sheet is for. Point only at:

1. **Their tier** — usually middle ($75 Growth). Say: *"Most businesses your size land here."*
2. **The "Coming soon" items** — *"These are rolling out through the year — you're grandfathered in as they ship, no price change."*
3. **The bottom of the sheet** — *"When you're ready, just text me Yes and I'll send your link."*

### Step 5: The ask (30 seconds)

Don't leave without asking. Pick one of these based on temperature:

- **Warm** (they're nodding, smiling): *"Want me to send you the invite link right now? You can fill it out tonight."*
- **Interested but hesitant**: *"How about this — I'll send you the link, no pressure. If you don't fill it out this week I'll follow up Monday. We haven't started charging the pilot group yet." (verbal pilot-period framing — don't commit to dates in writing)*
- **Polite no / needs to think**: *"Totally fair. Can I check back Friday? I'd really like you in the first 5."*

## Objection handlers

Prepare these — they WILL come up.

### "I already have a Facebook page"

> *"Great — Main Street OS actually posts to your Facebook for you, once we turn that on in a few weeks. The bigger thing is: Facebook shows your page to people who already follow you. This shows you to people asking questions about your category who've never heard of you. Totally different audience."*

### "I'm too busy to deal with another thing"

> *"That's exactly why I'm pitching this one. Signup is 2 minutes. After that, the AI does the work — it's showing your answer to customers whether you log in or not. The dashboard is just there if you want to see results. No social media calendar, no posting schedule. I genuinely wouldn't bring you something that needed your time."*

### "My customers don't use AI"

> *"Today, maybe. But 45% of consumers already use AI for local recommendations — up from 6% a year ago. It's moving fast. I'd rather have you featured now when it's still cheap than in 18 months when every business in Windom is trying to get in."*

### "What's the catch? Why so cheap?"

> *"No catch. I need 10-20 businesses to make the numbers work for the whole project. If I charged $500/month only two of you would sign up. Once we prove it out, honest guess is the pricing stays where it is — your price is locked for year one."*

### "Can I try it free first?"

> *"The pilot group actually is free for the first 30 days — I just need the signup so you can start collecting data. If you're not happy by day 30, you cancel, no charge."*

*(Only offer this if you want to. Don't if you've already planned differently.)*

### "Send me some info and I'll look at it"

Kiss of death — means no. Push back gently:

> *"I get that. Can I send you the signup link instead? That way if you do want to move forward you can do it in 2 minutes, and if you don't, nothing happens — it expires in a week."*

This converts "I'll look at it" to a one-click decision. If they say yes to the link, you have a 50/50 shot they'll use it. If you just send info, it's 5%.

---

## The close

Whether they say yes or no, end with:

> *"One more thing — whatever you decide, I'd love to know what you thought of the pitch. I'm testing this out on five of you and I want to get better at it. Can I text you Friday?"*

This does three things:
1. Gives reluctant folks a graceful way to say "yes to a conversation, no to a purchase"
2. Sets up a second touch without seeming pushy
3. Signals you're learning — makes them want to help you

- **A pen** — for writing their direct cell on the back of the one-pager if they say "text me Friday"

---

## What NOT to say

- "Artificial intelligence" — say "AI" or "the assistant"
- "SaaS" — say "monthly service" or "subscription"
- "Platform" — say "tool" or "program"
- "Leverage" / "optimize" / "drive" — just don't
- "I think you'd really benefit from..." — they decide benefits, not you
- Specific ROI numbers you can't back up — no "you'll get 10× return" claims

## After each visit (2 minutes, before you drive away)

Fill this in on your phone:

```
Business:     __________________________________
Result:       Yes / Maybe / No / Rescheduled
Tier chosen:  Starter / Growth / Premium / —
Objection:    ________________________________________
Follow-up:    What / When ___________________________
One thing I'd change about the pitch:
```

This is Sprint 1's real output: **what did we learn from 5 real conversations.** The build plan bends around that, not the other way.
